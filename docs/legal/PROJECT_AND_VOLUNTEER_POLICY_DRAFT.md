# Ayinou Project & Volunteer Policy

**DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION**

## 1. Purpose and eligibility

Projects let people propose and coordinate community work. The individual, institution and community submission paths each check their own reviewed identity, ownership and scope requirements. An individual eligible to submit a personal project may lack authority to sponsor a different owner's project. An institutional sponsor needs current institutional verification and sufficient authority. A community sponsor must act in the proper community. Projects can remain drafts, be returned for changes or be rejected where the relevant workflow permits. Participation in social features and an administrator bootstrap record do not substitute for project eligibility.

## 2. Proposal, review and visibility

Describe the purpose, community need, location at a safe level of detail, intended outcomes, responsible owner, material risks and planned role for volunteers. Label unconfirmed partners and estimates as such. Avoid precise addresses, sensitive participant details and links to private records. Reviewers evaluate eligible information against applicable scope, project fit and safety conditions. Corrections should be clear enough for a later reviewer to understand. An accepted proposal does not automatically appear in public. Publication depends on eligible recorded status and visibility. Restricted assignments and supporting records stay scoped.

## 3. Volunteering and qualification

Use clear opportunity descriptions, responsibilities, time expectations, supervision, location at an appropriate level and relevant qualifications. Applying does not guarantee a place. Offers and role assignments require appropriate acceptance. A participant should be able to decline an assignment beyond their competence or consent. A withdrawal from an opportunity can release its place and remove access derived from that opportunity. Follow instructions for safe handover of unfinished work or confidential records. Do not retaliate against someone for raising a concern or withdrawing.

Project roles confer bounded project authority. A member assigned to a task cannot assume institutional authority. Sensitive appointments depend on applicable identity and authorization checks. Regulated, hazardous or professional work requires separate real-world qualification, authorization, training and supervision. ## 4. Conflicts, evidence and outcomes

Disclose personal, financial or organizational interests that could affect proposal review, allocation or sign-off. Follow independent assignment and review procedures. Do not approve your own evidence where separation is required. Obtain permission for identifiable photos or testimonial use, use summaries rather than participant files, and remove sensitive details from images, links and text. Store restricted project records only in covered workflows. Projects may retain reviewed history and corrections. A public outcome should distinguish measured results from estimates, state its timeframe and method where possible and explain significant adverse or incomplete results. A published result can reflect a team's own account rather than independent validation. [Privacy Policy](PRIVACY_POLICY_DRAFT.md)

## 5. Fraud, disputes, funding and archival

Report false outcomes, misuse of identities or authority, fraudulent fundraising and safeguarding concerns using an available report path. Use the relevant project or moderation workflow for corrections and escalation. There is no guaranteed self-service reconsideration for project decisions. [Approval required — independent review, notice, safety escalation and handling of unresolved assignments and restricted evidence on archival.] Archival does not erase project history automatically.
