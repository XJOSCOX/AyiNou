# Proposed nonprofit board governance and delegation policy

**DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION**

Internal draft for the contemplated Texas entity. It is not a certificate, bylaw, board appointment or adopted delegation.

## Governance and independence

The future governing documents should define board composition and independence, director appointments, terms, removals, vacancies, meeting notice, quorum, records, and whether there are legal corporate members. Platform members are distinct from any corporate membership. The founder or executive can hold only powers conferred by adopted documents and recorded delegations. Access to a software administrator account does not make anyone a corporate director or officer. Reserve board oversight of mission, budget, compensation, related-party transactions, audits, major contracts, significant risk, succession and dissolution.

## Delegation, finance and emergency authority

Delegations should record who may act, by resource and limit, with start and end dates, reporting and revocation. Committees operate under written charters. Use independent review, separation of spending and reconciliation, authorization thresholds and secure access for financial and data operations. An emergency delegation should be narrow, time-limited, logged, escalated promptly and subject to independent later ratification where permitted. Do not derive corporate spending or appointment authority from an in-app advisory vote.

## Succession, safeguarding and dissolution

Maintain director and key-officer succession, secure credential and domain custody, access transfer, operational continuity and conflicts reporting. Assign independent oversight of safeguarding and risk, including third-party partners and people who cannot report through the usual chain. When considering dissolution, document liabilities, restricted assets, applicable approvals, creditor and beneficiary communications, custody of records and transfer of remaining assets under applicable law and adopted organizing documents.
