# Proposed nonprofit conflict-of-interest policy

**DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION**

Internal instrument for a future independent Texas nonprofit. Adoption would require counsel review and a recorded board vote after formation.

## Scope and disclosure

Directors, officers, key staff and delegated committee members disclose actual, potential and perceived conflicts at appointment, during periodic review and promptly before a specific decision. A conflict can arise from compensation, family or business relationships, ownership, grants, competing duties, institutional connections or control of shared assets. A discloser provides relevant facts without unnecessary private details. Uncertainty goes to an independent director or designated committee.

## Decision and recusal

A disinterested body decides whether the person may provide factual information and whether a conflict requires recusal. The interested person leaves deliberation and does not vote, control the process or use private authority to influence the result. Verify quorum and voting rules under approved governing documents. Use credible comparables and realistic alternatives for compensation, procurement, transfers and related-party transactions. Approve only a documented fair arrangement serving the nonprofit's purpose. Seek independent counsel where authority is unclear. Urgency is not a reason to approve self-dealing.

## Record and follow-up

Record the nature of the conflict, abstentions, participants, alternatives, votes and rationale. Protect disclosure records and monitor approved arrangements. Investigate material omissions with a fair opportunity to respond. Provide independent escalation through the [Whistleblower Policy](WHISTLEBLOWER_POLICY_DRAFT.md).
