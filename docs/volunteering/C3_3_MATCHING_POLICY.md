# C3.3 Matching policy

The database first checks the exact publication, approved project scope, active project execution, deadline, positions, current catalog definitions and native visibility. It applies standing, community and project eligibility and blocks in both directions. Only the owner's opted-in and available profile proceeds to personal compatibility. Organizer discovery additionally requires a verified native recruiter and the volunteer's separate discovery consent and visible, discoverable, non-private profile. Screening-flagged opportunities can show their notice in recommendations and are excluded from organizer discovery. The platform has no matching route for youth or regulated placement.

| Classification | Deterministic meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Strong compatibility | All indicated mandatory requirements and participation checks match and the evaluated inputs are sufficiently known. |
| Partial compatibility | There is no known mandatory mismatch, but a preference or relevant input is missing, uncertain, or unmet. |
| Additional requirements | A known mandatory constraint differs, an explicitly required skill or language is missing from the self-report, or organizer screening applies. |

A missing required selection means the system lacks that declaration. It makes no finding about ability. Missing years or duration evidence remain unknown. A beginner-friendly opportunity does not count optional experience against the volunteer. Weekly comparison uses the guaranteed lower end of a reported band for a positive explanation, its upper end to detect a known mismatch, and treats the interval between them as unknown. A known end date can be compared with requested weeks. Availability without a known end date is insufficient to assert a fixed future duration. Exact department preferences or nationwide preferences govern physical and hybrid compatibility. Worldwide remote works without a department selection.

Enabled signals and weights order only the current bounded recommendation page. Required weight is 3–9, preferred weight is 1–2, and strong/partial/additional grouping precedes all numeric ordering. A deterministic opportunity ID tie-break resolves equivalent signals. No numeric score leaves the database. No re-ranking changes the ability to apply or the human decision.
