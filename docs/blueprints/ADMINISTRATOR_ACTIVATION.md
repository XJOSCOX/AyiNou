# Administrator activation

**Owner approved and implemented in F1.1 source. Hosted deployment is pending.** The uniquely recognized Founder has direct final authority over Founder-reserved platform administrative actions. An Administrator appointment authorized by the Founder requires no second Administrator. This revision supersedes the earlier second-Administrator decision for this appointment alone. Other governance, succession, compromised-account recovery and independent review retain their own protections.

## Approved workflow

1. The active recognized Founder selects an eligible existing member. The current eligibility baseline requires active account status, independent Identity Verified and a verified authenticator.
2. The Founder confirms the exact appointment with a live session, recent MFA and a written reason. Ayinou binds the authorized decision to the targeted account and proposal.
3. Ayinou issues a short-lived, account-bound, one-time credential. Retain the existing F1 policy of a random 32-byte code, hashed verifier, expiry at the earlier of 24 hours or the proposal deadline, five failed credential attempts, and one-time disclosure to the issuing Founder. Deliver the opaque link and credential separately through a private channel.
4. The target signs in to their own account, enters the code, completes fresh MFA and explicitly accepts. Until then there is no Administrator assignment or green badge.
5. PostgreSQL rechecks all authorization and lifecycle conditions and atomically records acceptance, activates the Administrator role, consumes the credential and writes the audit records.
6. The active assignment supplies the green Administrator badge. The appointment cannot grant red Founder authority, blue independent verification or gold Founder Verified.

## F1 baseline and resolved conflict

F1 already provides scoped invitation issuance and redemption in source and disposable tests. Hosted F1 migration `20261121140000_private_privileged_workflows.sql` remains pending separate deployment approval. Pre-F1 O5 allows eligible administrators to create proposals and keeps generic execution closed.

- The F1 wrapper for `o5_proposal_command_for_api` limits new `appoint` proposals to the recognized Founder. It delegates to O5, which verifies the session, recent MFA, written reason, exact target, eligibility, governance version and idempotency under the shared transaction lock.
- O5's `approve` command writes `approved_at` and `approver_id` after an independent Administrator decision. `o5_proposal_state` treats absence of `approved_at` as pending. O5's proposal-only `accept` also depends on approval and creates no role.
- The conflict is in F1's `f1_appointment_for_api`. Its `status` readiness calculation requires `approved_at` and a currently eligible Administrator approver. Its `activate` branch repeats those requirements and rejects an approver who is the proposer or target. The invite can exist with only the Founder present, but the target cannot redeem it.

## F1.1 authorization, activation and compatibility

1. **Distinguish new direct decisions from legacy proposals.** F1.1 adds a private, constrained `authorization_mode` to the O5 proposal with an unchanged legacy default and a Founder-direct value permitted only for `appoint`. Stamp Founder-direct authorization server-side at confirmed proposal creation in the same transaction as the original written-reason receipt. Reuse the O5 wrapper, verified actor, request idempotency and expected-version flow. Never accept this marker from a client, populate `approver_id` with the Founder or synthesize an independent approval event. Keep pre-F1.1 proposals and decisions on their existing path. A legacy pending case can expire or be withdrawn and recreated explicitly. Do not backfill direct authorization or alter completed history.
2. **Change only readiness and redemption for a marked appointment.** Resolve the same persisted authorization rule in both F1 branches. Require the original Founder decision and receipt, exact invitation/proposal/target binding, active recognized Founder with existing eligibility, allowed governance version and an open proposal and invitation. Revalidate the target's current live account and session, reviewed identity, fresh MFA, code, attempts, expiry and explicit acceptance. A copied code or locator cannot redeem from a different account. Loss of Founder authority or Founder suspension blocks issue and redemption. Legacy appointments keep their independent approval check. All other O5 kinds retain their existing checks and execution gates.
3. **Keep a single successful activation.** Retain the shared advisory lock, row locks and uniqueness, and preserve the existing role-assignment, proposal acceptance, credential consumption and audit transaction. Tie the committed role grant and F1 acceptance/activation to the original Founder decision and written reason, proposal, invitation and accepting target. Do not write successful events on any rejected or rolled-back action. Exact creation retries reconcile to one proposal and receipt. Issuance retries cannot create another invitation or redisclose the code. Redemption retries cannot grant twice or duplicate final audit records.
4. **Project direct appointment status accurately.** Reuse `/admin`, `/activate/a/{id}` and current route families. Add a scoped mode discriminator only where projections need it. For direct appointments, remove the independent approval and generic proposal-only acceptance controls. Show awaiting target acceptance, unavailable/expired and activation based on persisted state and the actual role grant. Preserve legacy proposal displays, unrelated governance actions and four-language parity. Keep the explicit acceptance assertion at the service boundary and bind it to the atomically recorded SQL redemption.
5. **Preserve boundaries.** Keep ordinary and Founder identity proof, ballot and evidence access, legal publication, privacy operations, protected leadership status changes, succession and compromised-account recovery outside this change. Ordinary review still needs two eligible unconflicted reviewers. A separately assigned Founder can cast one vote. Keep the six-hour S2 policy, its promotion-time controls and existing 15-minute TOTP freshness distinct. F1.1 adds no S2 enforcement.

F1.1 is validated in disposable infrastructure. Hosted development still needs F1 `20261121140000`, then F1.1 `20261121141000`. Applying either requires a separate deployment decision.
