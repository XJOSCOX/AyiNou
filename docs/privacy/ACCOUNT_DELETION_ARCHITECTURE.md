# Account deletion architecture

**DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR IMPLEMENTATION OR PUBLICATION**

Closure restricts access and public visibility. Erasure applies a reviewed disposition to each copy and dependency. The formal case tracks both separately. Ordinary profile editing, sign-out, account restrictions and content moderation continue through their existing controls. The process below is proposed for L3.4.

L3.4 implements the proposal's read-only discovery, case versioning and a two-marker simulation only. The design below remains the dependency checklist for any future real operation, including the segments that the simulation does not implement. [Implemented scope](L3_4_IMPLEMENTATION_REPORT.md)

## Current dependencies and proposed disposition

| Resource | Observed behavior at the baseline | Required checkpoint before Auth deletion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Auth, profiles, codes and relationships | Auth deletion cascades to the member profile, private fields, settings and many linked rows. Referral relationships can disappear when either participant is deleted. Auth signup metadata and provider copies have independent handling. | Inventory Auth metadata and linked records. Separate the member's copy from another person's information, and assess aggregate suppression, reuse of identifiers, and recovery contact rules. |
| Platform administration | Phase 2E guards administrator changes and protects MFA factor deletion or downgrade under defined conditions. Those checks do not establish continuity through every Auth removal path. | Calculate effective administrators and needed reviewers from live roles, restrictions, verified identity and MFA. Block removal of the last eligible administrator until a separate authorized successor has accepted, signed in with MFA, and proved essential admin access. Never auto-promote, bypass independent review, or rely on bootstrap. |
| Institutions | Deleting the account can cascade representative memberships while leaving an institution and its public records in place. Verification and governance constrain changes of ownership or authority. | Check actual owner, delegated authority, custom roles, verification and disputes. Require the applicable governance review and accepted successor where continuity is needed. Preserve institution-owned data and logo. |
| Communities | Community owner references can become null. Existing member-created community ownership transfer has an offer and acceptance workflow with recent verification. Community deletion may conflict with posts and associated projects. | Resolve owner continuity through the existing transfer workflow and recheck active successor eligibility, private-group access and pending offers. Preserve the community and other members' records. |
| Posts, comments and safety | Auth-linked posts and comments can cascade. Removing a root comment can remove replies authored by other members. Moderation references and appeal protections can block certain deletes. Removing a parent can detach a post-media retention reference. | Check both directions of the dependency graph. Use a future approved tombstone, redaction or narrow evidence preservation when appropriate, with authorized holds copied to stable references before unlinking anything. Resolve pending moderator and appeal dependencies independently. |
| Verification and ambassador cases | Some applicant and organization-membership relationships cascade into applications, with dependent evidence, appeals and reviews. Other paths detach the actor. | Assign pending cases to an authorized independent handler. Preserve required evidence in a restricted store before cascade, independently redacting reporter, reviewer and other subjects' fields. Recheck consent and public projections. |
| Projects, volunteers and progress | Personal project ownership can cascade entire projects. The project has exactly one owner scope, and the regular workflow treats ownership as immutable. Other memberships cascade and tasks or milestones can lose an assignee. | Treat personal project ownership as a blocking dependency until a separately approved transfer, closure or preservation mechanism applies. Review applications, accepted commitments, task assignments, collaborator access, milestones and project polls. Keep institution/community ownership distinct from representative access. |
| Polls | Auth removal nulls the ballot voter link where applicable but can delete electorate rows. Project or other scope removal may cascade poll records. Nominee links and cached candidate presentation require separate review. | Preserve finalized counts, outcomes and quorum provenance without exposing choices. Review open polls and frozen electorates before unlinking. Do not add voters, recompute the frozen denominator from surviving rows, mutate finalized outcomes, or permit re-voting by identifier reuse. Apply safe nomination and projection handling. |
| Storage and historical project images | The three private buckets serve posts, member avatars and institution logos. Storage ownership metadata is not an Auth foreign key. Proposal/progress images are `private.project_images.image` bytes keyed by project and digest, with revision references that cascade and progress references that can block deletion. | Resolve trusted association and all references for each asset. Preserve held or shared files. Reconcile bucket variants through Storage APIs and database image bytes through their own reference checks. This corrects the project-media classification in the earlier inventory and retention matrix. |

### Ballot records

Freeze and compare permitted aggregate results and quorum evidence before and after any voter unlinking. Handle open polls through a versioned case checkpoint so concurrent voting, scope changes and closure cannot alter eligibility or permit duplicates. Future approved disposition should preserve the original electorate count and eligibility provenance in minimized scope-bound records. Use a separate, time-limited poll-scoped uniqueness reference only if needed during the approved voting/dispute window, and disable its use for cross-poll identity matching. A reviewer may see safe procedural evidence such as the applicable quorum rule and independently computed aggregate equality. They do not receive individual choices or an identity-to-ballot mapping. Treat restricted evidence about disputed outcomes separately from any own-ballot receipt. Require review of small cohorts and differencing before releasing any additional aggregate. ## Execution stages and recovery

1. **Validate the case and intent.** Bind the verified Supabase subject, current session, recent step-up and explicit action confirmation to the expected case version. Check whether closure, particular erasure, or a broader deletion was requested. Requests from delegates or recovered accounts follow the separate proof review. Capture a non-secret receipt and the applicable approved policy version. [Rights workflow](RIGHTS_WORKFLOW_ARCHITECTURE.md)
2. **Build a read-only plan.** Enumerate expected identifiers, current ownership, grants, invitations issued and received, cases, data lineage, provider calls and object references. Classify each item as erase, redact, detach, preserve with a reason, or block for disposition. Use restricted resource references in the plan and immutable audit records. Keep raw evidence, ballot selections, credentials and links out of both. The case journal and the verification receipts must survive the loss of the Auth UUID.
3. **Resolve continuity and preservation.** Complete and verify any transfers with an accepted successor. Route active cases and evidence to independent authorized reviewers and apply narrow, time-reviewed holds. Capture other parties' dependencies. Obtain independent authorization for destructive execution under the approved role policy. Pause if a hold, appeal, ownership conflict, last-administrator risk or missing legal rule prevents a safe outcome.
4. **Fence the subject and revalidate.** Immediately before execution, lock the case and relevant resource versions and validate decisions, holds, new obligations and proof. Install a durable closure fence that applies to Fastify, direct authenticated RPCs and worker paths, even during provider revocation delays. Revoke live sessions and outstanding sensitive actions, invalidate incoming and outgoing invitations and pending offers as applicable, close grants safely, and stop new uploads, grants and requests from racing the plan. If a material change requires a different plan or authority, pause on a new version and reapprove it. Allow cancellation before the irreversible boundary under the same case lock; later requests receive accurate status and escalation.
5. **Apply approved item dispositions.** Use ordered, narrowly scoped operations with stable per-item idempotency keys, an execution lease and generation checks. Commit the step result with the database change where possible. For external actions, record intent before dispatch and verify using the provider's read-back or ledger. A timeout with unclear outcome enters reconciliation. After a partial failure, remain fenced; retry only an eligible unfinished step after checking live holds, version and scope. Restoration of erased data is not a recovery strategy.
6. **Finalize Auth last.** Recalculate expected cascades and remaining restrict constraints. Require positive readiness from reviewed grants, storage and evidence checkpoints and provider Auth deletion prerequisites. Use a separately permissioned Auth administrator operation tied to the case. Record confirmation through a privileged read-back and verify revocation, lack of new tokens/visibility, and completion of dependent denials. Keep separate statuses for provider material or backup copies still subject to approved rotation. Attach a minimized completion receipt through a verified channel that can work after account removal. Failed verification or unconfirmed obligations stay open for reconciliation.

## Storage authorization and restore

- Derive paths from server-validated asset records, bucket allowlists and normalized exact keys. Member avatars use a subject path, logos use a distinct institution path, and post variants use an attachment path. Never accept a requester-supplied bucket/key for deletion or infer ownership from provider metadata alone. Preserve translated alt text and any copied variants according to their own dispositions.
- Stop issuing media URLs before removal. Feed avatar links can remain valid for up to 120 seconds and own-profile/avatar and logo links can remain valid for up to 300 seconds. Post media already uses an authenticated read proxy. Previously downloaded bytes are outside server recall. Use an approved restricted evidence boundary for held derivatives and explicitly record outstanding access windows where they exist.
- Inventory attached, reserved, unattached and detached uploads, including both thumbnail and display variants and any lease or retention anchor. A bucket entry without a matching live attachment is a reconciliation candidate. Verify it against reservations, historical references, retry records and applicable holds before scheduling removal. For project bytes, enumerate all revision and progress references under the same project/digest, including private evidence and approved historical records.
- For storage, record expected ownership, variants, version and hold checks in the same eligibility journal. Establish the deletion fence before dispatch so new evidence cannot be linked silently during removal. Reconcile missing keys, provider timeouts, duplicate operations and objects with incomplete metadata through a separate review queue. Validate that active users and institutions keep their assets and the erased object is no longer accessible. - Restore to quarantine. Reapply signed external deletion markers and held-resource dispositions, validate Auth, database and media separately, and require independent release before re-enabling traffic.
