# Legal L3.4 account lifecycle simulation

Baseline `053223af6a8fae28cbae2edca1580638af6a87a1` on `main`. L3.4 adds read-only planning and simulation for received closure and deletion cases. L3.2 intake, L3.3 exports and L3.4 planning all remain disabled for real users. The application has no real closure, Auth-user removal, Storage deletion, account restriction or retention enforcement path for these cases.

## Dependency inventory and dispositions

The private plan snapshot inventories capped single-column Auth foreign-key impacts in the application schemas and explicitly checks higher-risk ownership, shared-content and media relationships. Catalog-sourced identifiers are quoted, and the server-verified subject is bound separately. It reads relationship counts and typed reference metadata, never raw evidence, account secrets or third-party content. The plan exposes coarse warning codes and a safe receipt to the subject. The catalog listing is a gap inventory, not a recursive execution DAG. Indirect references, provider custody and domain-specific legal exceptions need separate review for any production disposition.

| Domain | Planned checks and disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Account and governance | Catalog records direct auth relationship actions and potential cascades. Live Supabase sessions and recent TOTP bind sensitive commands. The last eligible platform administrator, other privileged roles and verification reviewers block simulation pending independent continuity decisions. No replacement is appointed. |
| Institutions and communities | Active institutional ownership, official representatives and custom roles, group owners and staff require continuity review. Ordinary affiliation, membership, grant, invitation and request dependencies are included through direct-role or catalog checks. Existing transfer workflows remain independent and a changed owner makes a previous snapshot stale. |
| Discussions | Own posts and comments propose redaction review; a root with other authors' replies or an owned post with other authors' comments is a blocker. Preserve open reports and moderation appeals. L3.4 does not introduce Feed tombstones or data rewrites. |
| Projects | Personally owned projects block execution under the immutable ownership contract. Preserve shared projects and inventory related revisions, applications, outstanding commitments, tasks, milestones, progress and reports. Flags for evidence and shared images prevent simulated progress. |
| Voting | A single integrity warning covers subject-linked voting dependencies. No vote IDs, selections, option IDs, counts, results, or electorate members are returned. The worker cannot alter votes, electorates, quorum data, nominees or results. |
| Media and evidence | The internal snapshot derives exact current avatar, institution logo and attachment variant paths from authorized references, not object filenames. It records detached or held attachment metadata, and recognizes both current and historical project image references. Project image bytes remain in PostgreSQL. No media bytes, signed URLs or paths enter the claimant response. |

Dispositions are recommendations for review, including redaction, preservation, transfer, possible identity detachment and blocking. Catalog counts cap at 129, and reaching the bound blocks progress. Clean plans (without blockers or holds) can be approved only for the fixed synthetic steps below. Support for a closure case does not imply an operational closure action.

## Authorization, synthetic holds and policy

Fastify requires `AYINOU_PRIVACY_LIFECYCLE=synthetic` in disposable loopback with L3.2 synthetic intake enabled. PostgreSQL checks both initially empty fixture allowlists independently. Plans lock scope and case version and record a sanitized source snapshot. Repeating a save with its idempotency key reuses its receipt. Confirmation is separately versioned and idempotent. Account Settings uses uncached in-memory state in Haitian Creole, French, English and Spanish.

Independent case-scoped authorities cover approval, hold placement, hold release, simulation and restore. Their grants are seeded only by the isolated database owner and carry an expiry and conflict marker. A subject cannot grant themselves authority or approve their own case. Platform or organization roles convey no lifecycle privileges. A hold references the original plan and an exactly inventoried scope/reference. Holds record reason, rule version, placer, review date and independent release. Every active hold for the subject prevents simulated progress. Releasing one of two holds leaves the other effective, and closing a case or passing its review date releases neither. Only the synthetic policy is installed. Its retention duration and approval reference are null and its destruction flag is false. The synthetic hold review date is a test scheduling input only.

## Execution, reconciliation and restore

The operator RPC has no Auth, Storage, external provider or product-domain write adapter. Only two fictional markers can change. Before each step, it checks the live session, current MFA, case/version, independent approval, latest snapshot and every active hold. A 30-second lease fences workers. A lost response after an effect requires read-back against the marker. An expired claim can be reconciled even if the claim response and its lease token were lost. The read-back must verify absence or explicitly record retryability before another effect can run. Effects and audit events are transactional. Partial state remains visible until both markers independently verify. Usage limits and key reuse prevent duplicated test effects. A changed source or newly placed hold stops progress while the case remains reviewable. The simulation locks lifecycle records, with no account-wide write fence or real account mutation.

The restore scenario deliberately resurrects the two synthetic markers under quarantine, reapplies the previously verified suppression state using a scoped restore operator, and requires separate release. This exercises the state machine in isolated PostgreSQL. It does not restore an actual production or Supabase backup. Real restore requires an independently protected erasure ledger, provider-specific backup and Storage controls, Auth and media consistency checks, hold restoration, and verified prevention of reappearance before admitting traffic.

## Remaining legal and operational gates

Actual closure or erasure requires approved legal authority, category schedules and hold exceptions, independently assigned review and succession, verified upstream dependencies, executable provider/Auth/Storage contracts, and independently reviewed incident and restoration processes. L3.5 can use this simulation foundation under separate authorization. It must validate production operational procedures without activating retention or erasure. No public legal policy was published.
