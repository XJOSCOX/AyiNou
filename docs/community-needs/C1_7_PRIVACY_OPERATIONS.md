# C1.7 Community Needs privacy operations

| Data | Default custody and rights work |
| --- | --- |
| Private report and original revisions | Author and specifically authorized operational actors. Export only the requester’s approved material after identity verification and third-party redaction. Preserve revision integrity under a hold |
| Public reference metadata | Treat URLs and titles as private case data. Screen for personal information, credentials, and identifying query strings. Community snapshots do not contain raw source references |
| Reviewer notes and findings | Restricted evidence and reasoned decisions, including supported and adverse outcomes. Separate member-facing findings from internal notes and third-party material |
| Publication snapshot and attribution consent | Release only the approved community projection. Attribution defaults to private. Withdrawal suppresses visibility per native rules, with protected history retained where approved |
| Project association | Keep consent, exact version pointers and unlink history separate from the visible project projection |
| Private notices | Minimize content and use structured references. Keep notices inside authenticated channels. Support must avoid sending case details by unapproved email or chat |
| Audit history and attestations | Preserve the minimum logical identifiers, timestamps, decisions and reason appropriate for audit and appeal. Keep underlying artifacts with their authorized custodians |

Use the existing privacy intake, export, lifecycle and hold tooling as a basis. The completed flows are synthetic. Qualified privacy or legal review must approve purpose and lawful basis, translated notices, age/eligibility and accessibility safeguards, departmental scope, retention triggers and periods, response deadlines, exceptions, delivery, de-identification standards, hold authority, appeal safeguards and deletion from backups. An approved retention schedule must distinguish withdrawn drafts, active reports, findings, adverse outcomes, challenges, consent, published data, audits and restoration copies. No retention period is assumed.

Procedure before handling any real request:

1. Open a confidential record in the approved rights channel. Verify requester identity proportionately, document scope and deadlines and restrict assignment to authorized staff.
2. Map the requester’s records and affected department, revisions, projections, objects, links, notices and dependencies. Check open challenges, legal or evidence holds and third-party risks. Route holds to the authorized custodian and distinguish dispute preservation from publication.
3. Determine access, correction, export, erasure or restriction disposition. Redact third parties and privileged notes, separate internal evidence, and use a second authorized check. Deliver through an approved expiring access channel. Use the native process for withdrawal and projection invalidation.
4. Where qualified review approves deletion, verify holds and appeal obligations again first. Minimize or de-identify if preservation is required. Record disposition and reconcile all copies, restoration suppression and remaining references. Communications must use the member’s chosen supported language.
5. Apply one approved national privacy baseline with scope-aware handling in each department. Keep case ownership and access scoped to the relevant community. Restrict central dashboards to aggregates and suppress small cells. Coordinate multi-department requests without merging unrelated case material. After restoration reconcile permissions, later holds, consent withdrawals, suppressed data and deletions before reopening access.
